Reading this as a graph

Treat every MP, party, ministry and topic as a node and every question as an edge, and three questions become answerable that a flat pivot table can't reach: which ministries are a party's genuine signature rather than just its biggest by volume; which parties cluster together purely by what they ask about, regardless of stated ideology; and where MPs cross party lines to co-sign a question.

Signature ministries — party focus network

Each edge is a party–ministry pair where that party asks disproportionately more than its overall share of questions would predict (lift ≥ 1.5, at least 15 questions) — not just its biggest ministries by raw count. Party nodes are coloured by the community they fall into below. Drag nodes; hover for detail.

Party communities — who clusters with whom, by what they ask

Louvain community detection on party–ministry lift vectors (cosine similarity > 0.15, parties with ≥30 questions only): two parties land together when their relative emphasis across ministries is similar, independent of how many questions either asks in total.

Ministry scheme catalogues vs. PQ scrutiny

MeitY, DoT and DPIIT run JS-shell sites with no crawlable page content, but each serves its scheme catalogue underneath through a public WordPress-backed API — two different API shapes across the three, verified independently rather than assumed identical. Each scheme is matched against PQ text by its acronym (only when genuinely scheme-specific) or distinguishing title words — a heuristic match, a lower bound, not proof of zero attention where it shows 0.
